# Effects

Every track has its own insert effects and two sends; the sends feed two buses (delay and reverb), and everything leaves through the master EQ, compressor and tape saturation.

```text
engine → DRIVE → CRUSH → FILTER → CHORUS → track fader ─┬─→ out
                                                       ├─ DELAY send ─→ master delay ─┐
                                                       └─ REVERB send → master reverb ┴→ EQ → COMP → TAPE → out
```

## Master effects {#fx-master}

TAPE's FX page, part of the project (saved since project format v4):

<Shot name="tape-fx">
  <Mark at="cells:1,1-1,4">DELAY: tempo-synced tape delay</Mark>
  <Mark at="cells:2,1-2,4">REVERB: 4-line FDN, on the dark side</Mark>
  <Mark at="cell:3,1">COMP: bus compressor</Mark>
  <Mark at="cells:3,2-3,3">EQ: low / high shelf ±12 dB</Mark>
  <Mark at="cell:3,4">TAPE: tape saturation</Mark>
  <Mark at="cells:4,1-4,4">Echoes at the current tempo, reverb decay</Mark>
  <Caption>Master FX.</Caption>
</Shot>

- **DELAY**: TIME is a note value from 1/32 to 1/2 (default 1/8D), synced to the BPM. Its feedback loop has a TONE low-pass and an 80 Hz high-pass; changing the note value glides like tape. Echoes up to 743 ms use a 16 KB line; a longer time takes a 32 KB line (up to 1.49 s), and if that does not fit the time is halved until it does.
- **REVERB**: about 4.5 kHz wide, on the dark side.
- Both sends are decimated to 11.025 kHz before processing, so delay and reverb sound warm and a little lo-fi.

<Keywords k="master, bus, tape delay, reverb, eq, compressor, saturation, 总线" />

## Memory and CPU {#fx-cpu}

Effect buffers are taken the first time an effect is used and given back when it is not: the delay line 16 KB (32 KB for echoes longer than 743 ms) and the reverb about 8.8 KB, both in the device's 2 MB PSRAM, and 1 KB per track for its chorus.

- If the delay or reverb cannot get memory, that bus is silent and the FX page and the signal chain show NO MEM. With every effect off, the output is bit-identical to having no effects.
- Each track plays a few notes at once: DX7 4, JUNO and DRUMS 6, SAMPLER 4. When all are busy, a new note takes the voice of an older one, a released one first.
- Audio has a budget of 5.8 ms per 256 frames, shared by both of the chip's cores. When a block would not fit, the device fades out the notes that matter least: always on the track with the most notes sounding, its released tails first, then its quietest held note. A track only ever gives way to itself, and the beat never slows or stops.

:::warning

Very dense chords with many effects can crackle: play fewer notes at once, or turn an effect or two off.

:::
